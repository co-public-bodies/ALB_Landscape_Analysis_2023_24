<p><b> FTE in post on 31st March 2024 </b><br>
This captures the number of staff in post (expressed as Full-time equivalent (FTE)) on the 31st of March 2024. This data excludes vacancies and contingent labour. We asked for FTE as of the 31st of March to align our data with the workforce data captured by the <a href="https://www.gov.uk/government/statistics/civil-service-statistics-2024/quality-and-methodology-information-for-civil-service-statistics-2024">Annual Civil Service Employment Survey (ACSES)</a>.</p>

<p><b> Staff who are on maternity/paternity leave: </b><br>
For staff on maternity/paternity leave - we asked for staff who are on paid maternity leave to be included in ALB FTE values returned, but for staff who are off on unpaid leave, we asked for them to not be included in these values.</p>

<p><b> Contingent Labour </b><br>
To allow for comparisons with ALBs' core workforces, we captured the number of contingent labour staff employed by ALBs as of the 31st of March 2024, expressed as FTE. </p> <p>By Contingent Labour we refer to staff such as contractors, agency workers, and temps, who are employed on a temporary basis. They will often, but not always, be recruited to undertake work in a business as usual (BAU) environment. These contracts will generally pay on a time-based rate rather than delivery of an output or outcome, where the staff will be expected to complete specific tasks or services under a 'Statement of Work'.</p> <p>This differs from consultancy. Contingent Labour are typically external employees who are included on payroll, either directly or indirectly through an intermediary, such as an agency. Consultancy is typically defined as the purchase of advice to fill a knowledge gap. This can include identification of options and recommendations, or assisting with implementing solutions, but should not be business as usual (BAU). Consultancy spend is typically associated with a specific deliverable, and consultants are not typically included in payroll.</p>
